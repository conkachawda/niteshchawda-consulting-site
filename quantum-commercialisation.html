University of Sussex / Academic case study

From quantum research
to commercial value.

How can policymakers and entrepreneurs turn scientific progress into a commercially sustainable industry?

All education & ideas
Setting
University of Sussex
Module
Emerging Markets for Quantum Technology
Work type
Academic analysis · proposed interventions

Scientific progress alone does not create a viable market. Emerging technologies also need customers, commercial leadership, manufacturing capability and a workforce ready to adopt them.

Five connected levers

01 / Create early demand

Connect technical development to credible customer needs. Early procurement and lead customers can reduce uncertainty and help a new technology move towards useful applications.

02 / Build commercial leadership

Align capital allocation and leadership capability with the path to market. Funding decisions need to reflect adoption, execution and commercial risk as well as technical promise.

03 / Develop industrial capability

Manufacturing, infrastructure and skills must develop together. A promising laboratory result needs an ecosystem capable of delivering it consistently.

04 / Address standards and sovereignty

Consider how standards, trusted supply chains and national capability influence the choices available to policymakers and entrepreneurs.

05 / Enable the adopting organisation

Build the workforce’s ability to recognise, evaluate and apply the technology. The customer’s capacity to adopt is part of the commercialisation problem.

The central finding

Commercialisation is a system. Build the capability to adopt a technology alongside the technology itself, and test the value of each intervention before scaling it.

How I tested the thinking

I brought innovation-systems literature together with implementation proposals, delivery responsibilities, risk mitigations and illustrative financial scenarios, including discounted cash-flow analysis.

The models are decision tools based on assumptions. They are not evidence of realised returns or a delivered client programme.

Why it matters to my approach

Education creates understanding. Enablement builds the capacity to act. The big rocks identify the barriers, and the financial case helps decide which interventions deserve a bounded proof.
